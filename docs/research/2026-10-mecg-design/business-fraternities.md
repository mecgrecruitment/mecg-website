# Michigan business fraternity design benchmarks

Observed 2026-10-17. Primary chapter sites only. Desktop visual inspection at 1280 × 800 plus page text and selected computed styles. This is a design comparison, not a prestige or organizational quality ranking. No private portals were accessed; conclusions about their private UX would be unsupported.

## Alpha Kappa Psi — Phi Chapter

**Observed:** The live [homepage](https://www.akpsi-phi.com/) uses a full-screen blue, navy and pale-gold flowing abstract image, a large white Greek-letter wordmark with a gold stroke, white navigation and small capsule actions. Inter is the computed interface font; the welcome heading is 48px navy. Navigation is compact: Home, About Us, Our Members, Rush, Sign In. A real president portrait and personal letter introduce the people below the hero. The [about page](https://www.akpsi-phi.com/about) separates community, leadership, network and development, with photos and an FAQ. The [rush page](https://www.akpsi-phi.com/rush) lists dates, times, locations, event explanations and an Apply action. Its indexed timeline contains a weekday/date inconsistency, so it is a useful structural reference rather than a calendar authority.

**Inference:** This is the most visually distinctive fraternity homepage of the three inspected: richness comes from a single immersive material image and recognizable identity. Its F26 hero action requires insider interpretation. MECG can borrow the visual confidence while spelling out “Fall recruitment” and showing the current state.

## Delta Sigma Pi — Xi Chapter

**Observed:** The [homepage](https://www.dspxi.com/) has a white header, navy identity, dark-red widely spaced navigation and an oversized dark-red recruitment headline over a real chapter photo. Computed styles include navy `#080451` and dark red `#8b0000`; rendered heading color is applied in nested spans. White photo treatment lightens the image behind the copy. Typography mixes display capitals, a serif-like supporting line and sans-serif body styles. The first screen explicitly says it serves Ross business students and links to Fall 2026 rush information. The [rush page](https://www.dspxi.com/rush) pairs social photographs with a schedule and interest form; “application coming soon” remains beside dates that have passed as of inspection. The [alumni page](https://www.dspxi.com/alumni) has an employer image and class-by-class alumni lists.

**Inference:** The strongest lesson is immediate eligibility and action clarity, with visible real people. Large type plus pale photo wash competes with the people rather than presenting work evidence. MECG should use decisive headings on a clean ground beside photos, and automatically retire expired recruitment calls to action.

## Phi Chi Theta — Zeta-Beta Chapter

**Observed:** The functioning chapter domain is [umichpct.com](https://www.umichpct.com/); the alternate pctumich.com host failed SSL in this audit. A white header carries crest and name, with Who We Are, Leadership, Rush and Apply Now. A full-width real chapter photograph dominates the hero; gray title/subtitle and a purple outlined capsule interest button sit over it. White/gray, purple and crest gold provide identity. Computed welcome typography is Lato, 36px heading and 15px body. The [about page](https://www.umichpct.com/about) gives concrete development activities, community events, service, DEI and growth, plus member/alumni placements and named testimonials. It self-reports membership, academic breadth and employment statistics without a visible methodology; these are chapter claims, not independently established outcomes. The [recruitment page](https://www.umichpct.com/work/rush) gives practical preparation guidance, including when a resume is needed.

**Inference:** PCT makes belonging tangible through people and specific activities, and reduces applicant anxiety through preparation copy. The photographic hero makes the chapter feel real, but text sits on a visually busy image. MECG can gain the warmth with separate captioned photos and succinct, directly answerable applicant questions.

## Transferable design principles for MECG

1. **Show actual people at useful scale.** Campus peers make belonging visible. Give MECG equally strong people photography, alongside client-work artifacts that establish its different purpose.
2. **Use one strong identity gesture.** AKPsi demonstrates that a restrained interface can still have colour and life through a large, specific visual. MECG's blue/orange can do more than tint small badges.
3. **Make eligibility and next steps plain.** Say all majors, what preparation matters, when the next step happens, and whether applications are currently open. Avoid semester abbreviations as the only button label.
4. **Replace generic status claims with specific evidence.** Mentoring activities, actual project roles, a real artifact and a named story are more informative than copying “premier” or an unexplained employer logo wall.
5. **Be warmer and clearer than the recurring template.** All three use a large identity/recruitment hero followed by a long president letter. MECG can lead with its work, its people and two clear audience routes, while keeping a short signed welcome deeper in the site.
6. **Let the public identity enter private workspaces selectively.** Bring photos/avatars, confident blue sections and purposeful orange actions into onboarding and home screens. Keep review tables, forms and decision states quiet and legible. This is a recommendation; no fraternity private UI was observed.
