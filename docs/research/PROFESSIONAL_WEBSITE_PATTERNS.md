# Professional website patterns for the MECG redesign

Research date: September 1, 2026
Scope: Current first-party websites from strategy consulting, engineering and design consulting, and interactive production studios.

## Recommendation

MECG should look like a small, rigorous consulting organization with access to exceptional student talent. It should not imitate a global firm's scale or an interactive studio's spectacle.

The strongest direction combines three ideas:

- Use real MECG people and project environments as the main visual material.
- Put evidence close to every major claim. Show named outcomes when permitted, the method used, the team involved, and the date.
- Reserve one restrained 3D scene for the homepage. The scene should explain how MECG turns a complex situation into a decision, not act as a decorative object.

This is a synthesis from the observed patterns below. It is a recommendation, not a claim that any one reference uses this exact combination.

## What the references do

### Strategy consulting

#### McKinsey & Company

Sources: [homepage](https://www.mckinsey.com/), [about](https://www.mckinsey.com/about-us/overview), and [United States work](https://www.mckinsey.com/us/our-work)

Verified observations:

- The homepage opens with one short proposition, then moves into current research, client capabilities, careers, and social impact.
- Content carries a visible type such as survey, article, blog, podcast, or webinar. The labels help visitors scan a large library.
- Service language stays tied to client action. The homepage divides help into industry expertise, capabilities, and technology and AI.
- The United States section uses case studies and named partnerships to make local impact concrete.

Transfer to MECG: Keep the homepage message short. Label content and proof by type, then let visitors choose between work, services, and joining the team.

#### Boston Consulting Group

Sources: [homepage](https://www.bcg.com/), [about](https://www.bcg.com/about/overview), and [client impact](https://www.bcg.com/about/client-impact)

Verified observations:

- The homepage leads with a clear position, "Where Strategic Clarity Meets Applied AI," rather than a list of services.
- Client impact has its own high-level path. It appears before the larger stream of current insights on the homepage.
- The about page presents scale and performance figures together, including employees, revenue, service breadth, and a comparative client-return figure.
- BCG Answer turns the firm's research library into an interactive question entry point.

Transfer to MECG: Give "Our work" equal weight with "What we do." Use only verified figures and state what each figure measures. A small guided project finder could be useful later, but a generic AI chat box would add little before MECG has a deep content library.

### Engineering and design consulting

#### Arup

Sources: [homepage](https://www.arup.com/en-us/), [projects](https://www.arup.com/en-us/projects/), and [WS2 project](https://www.arup.com/en-us/projects/ws2/)

Verified observations:

- The homepage mixes projects, current issues, and employee portraits with names, roles, locations, and first-person quotes.
- The projects page organizes work by sectors such as transport, energy, water, property, and data centers.
- The WS2 project page starts with location, market, and client. It then combines credited photography, a video, partner names, service tags, quotes, and a short "What we delivered" summary.
- The WS2 photography shows several views of one place instead of repeating one hero image.

Transfer to MECG: Build case studies as evidence packages. Each package needs context, MECG's role, the process, the result, the team, and several relevant images. Use captions to explain why each image matters.

#### WSP

Sources: [homepage](https://www.wsp.com/en-us), [projects](https://www.wsp.com/en-us/projects), [about](https://www.wsp.com/en-us/who-we-are), and [impact](https://www.wsp.com/en-us/our-impact)

Verified observations:

- The homepage pairs two scale figures with a sector list and a geographically varied project selection.
- Project cards include a place before the project name. This makes a large global portfolio easier to understand.
- The impact page uses project stories, video, community themes, and a direct invitation to discuss a project.
- The services area connects service categories with related projects and insights instead of leaving services as an isolated directory.

Transfer to MECG: Add project context directly to cards. A useful MECG card can show organization type, challenge, engagement type, semester, and verified result. Connect each service page to relevant work and people.

#### IDEO

Sources: [homepage](https://www.ideo.com/), [about](https://www.ideo.com/about), and [work](https://www.ideo.com/work)

Verified observations:

- The homepage uses changing words within its opening statement, then immediately explains the offer in one sentence.
- Featured work names recognizable clients and describes the change made, not only the design discipline used.
- Named client testimonials sit near the firm's experience claim. The quotes describe organizational or commercial change.
- The about page uses milestone work to tell the firm's history, including Apple, PillPack, and Ford.

Transfer to MECG: Use a short, specific verbal system in the hero, but avoid an endless rotating word effect. Ask partners for outcome-focused testimonials. Turn MECG's history into a few dated projects or milestones instead of a long biography.

#### frog

Sources: [homepage](https://www.frog.co/), [work](https://www.frog.co/work), and [services](https://www.frog.co/services)

Verified observations:

- The homepage opens with a small set of current stories. One item has an explicit play control, while other items lead to articles or reports.
- Work, services, people, insights, and contact each have distinct sections rather than appearing in one undifferentiated card grid.
- The page changes tone between commercial work, research, and people while keeping one navigation system.
- The contact invitation states the types of work the firm can make and scale.

Transfer to MECG: Give each homepage section one job. Use video only when it carries a project story or a clear explanation. Write contact calls to action around a real next step, such as discussing a project or requesting a team.

#### Pentagram

Sources: [homepage](https://www.pentagram.com/), [work](https://www.pentagram.com/work), and [news](https://www.pentagram.com/news)

Verified observations:

- The site treats project imagery as the primary navigation into the work.
- The work index supports filters for client sector, discipline, location, and year.
- Project descriptions identify the client and the design result in one line.
- News and work stay separate. News carries a type and date, while work remains a durable portfolio.

Transfer to MECG: Keep project stories separate from announcements. Add filters only after MECG has enough cases to make them useful. Strong, consistent project thumbnails will improve the site more than a complex filter on a small portfolio.

### Interactive and motion studios

#### BUCK

Sources: [homepage](https://buck.co/), [work index](https://www.buck.co/work?tag=featured), and [Comfy brand refresh](https://buck.co/work/comfy-brand-refresh)

Verified observations:

- The homepage is unusually selective. A one-sentence description and three featured projects carry most of the page.
- The work index labels projects by format, including 2D, 3D, branding, live action, systems, and experience.
- The Comfy case study derives its spatial forms and motion from the client product's node-based logic. The shapes connect, separate, expand, and reorganize to reflect how inputs become a complex result.
- The case study separates the core visual system, typography and color, motion, and credits. It explains the reason for each part.

Transfer to MECG: Derive motion from MECG's actual work. The 3D scene should represent evidence, alternatives, constraints, and a decision. Do not import a generic glowing mesh, glass orb, or floating gradient merely because it looks technical.

#### UNIT9

Sources: [homepage](https://unit9.com/), [digital work](https://www.unit9.com/digital), [Spatial by UNIT9](https://spatial.unit9.com/), and [Verizon: The Met Replica](https://www.unit9.com/project/verizon-met-replica)

Verified observations:

- The main work taxonomy separates interactive, film, and experiential work. Deeper pages add games, virtual and augmented reality, and digital work.
- Spatial by UNIT9 states principles before showing experiments and client work. Its principles cover participation, context, multiple input methods, and accessible levels of immersion.
- The Met Replica case study explains the audience, the two-part experience, the technology, the interaction, the result, and the credits.
- The Met project uses 3D to represent a real institution and its collection. The digital form follows the subject matter.

Transfer to MECG: Treat 3D as a designed interaction with a reason to exist. Provide a complete non-3D route to the same information. The scene must still communicate when the visitor does not drag, hover, or use a precise pointer.

## Patterns worth adopting

### Lead with a position, then prove it

McKinsey, BCG, IDEO, and BUCK all compress the opening message. Their sites save detail for work, capabilities, or case studies. MECG's homepage should state who it helps, what it changes, and why its model is credible in no more than two short sentences.

Recommended order for the MECG homepage:

1. Position and primary action.
2. Selected work with verified results.
3. The engagement model, shown as a clear process.
4. Services tied to relevant cases.
5. People and partner testimony.
6. A current insight or event.
7. A project inquiry and a separate student recruitment action.

### Make the photography factual

Arup is the best reference here. Its project photography documents the place, materials, people, and result. Employee images also carry a name, role, and personal point of view. That combination feels authored because every image belongs to a specific story.

Commission a first MECG library of 18 to 24 original images:

- One environmental group portrait that shows MECG in its University of Michigan setting.
- Four to six working-session images with students, partners, whiteboards, prototypes, or analysis in view.
- Six to eight project images, with partner approval. Capture the situation, the work product, and the result.
- Consistent portraits for the current leadership team.
- Two to four campus or event images that establish place without becoming generic university promotion.

Give each image a planned use, caption, credit, consent record, and alt text. Avoid stock office scenes, fake meeting poses, portraits with unrelated lighting styles, and AI-generated people. If client privacy prevents photography, show an approved artifact, diagram, or anonymized detail and say what it is.

### Use a proof hierarchy

The reference sites do not rely on logo walls alone. They combine client work, scale figures, testimonials, people, methods, and current thinking.

MECG should rank proof in this order:

1. A verified project result with a date and scope.
2. A named partner quote with title and organization, when permission exists.
3. The concrete deliverable and method.
4. The named MECG team or project lead.
5. A client logo or broad participation figure.

Never display an unverified number as visual filler. If a result cannot be published, state the nature of the work and the reason for anonymization.

### Give the 3D scene an MECG subject

Recommended concept: "The decision model."

The scene begins as a field of evidence points, operating constraints, and competing options. Three visual families reflect MECG's work in strategy, operations, and technology. As the visitor scrolls, the field becomes a readable decision path and ends in one resolved plan with measurable checkpoints.

The visual language should come from consulting work:

- Thin paths resemble process maps and system diagrams.
- Nodes represent evidence or stakeholders, not arbitrary particles.
- Planes resemble working documents, models, and decision tables.
- A restrained Michigan and MECG palette ties the scene to the organization.
- Small labels use real terms such as evidence, constraint, option, decision, and measure.

The scene should have four states: orient, compare, decide, and measure. Each state needs one sentence of copy and one clear camera position. Scroll can advance states, but the page must also provide visible controls and a static fallback.

Implementation guardrails:

- Use one 3D scene on the public site at first. Do not repeat WebGL on service and case-study pages.
- Load a static poster immediately. Start the interactive scene after the main content is usable.
- Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`. Reduced motion should switch states without camera travel or object drift.
- Provide the same message in HTML outside the canvas.
- Stop rendering when the canvas is outside the viewport.
- Test keyboard navigation, touch, low-power mobile devices, and zoom before launch.
- Define a performance budget before production. Measure it on representative phones instead of choosing a library first.

The final two bullets are implementation recommendations. The public pages reviewed here do not provide enough data to infer their performance budgets or reduced-motion behavior.

## Features that would improve the site

Prioritize these features after the visual foundation:

1. A case-study template with challenge, approach, deliverables, results, team, semester, and related services.
2. A compact project finder filtered by service and organization type. Add it only when enough published cases exist.
3. Service pages that automatically surface related projects, people, and insights.
4. A "How an engagement works" timeline with responsibilities and expected outputs at each stage.
5. A partner inquiry flow that asks about the problem, timing, organization, and contact details without becoming a long intake form.
6. A student recruiting path with dates, expectations, selection steps, and current status.
7. A small insights library for original MECG analysis. Every item needs an author, publication date, and reading time.
8. A persistent "Discuss a project" action on project and service pages. Keep "Join MECG" separate.
9. An accessibility and motion control that remains available after the hero.

## Patterns to avoid

- Do not copy the content volume of McKinsey or BCG. MECG does not need a news feed that makes the best work harder to find.
- Do not copy the dense service directory on a global engineering site. Start with the few offers MECG can explain and prove.
- Do not make every section move. Motion loses meaning when headings, cards, counters, cursors, and backgrounds all compete.
- Do not build the homepage around a reel. A prospective partner needs to understand the offer without watching video.
- Do not use 3D as a trust substitute. Client outcomes, people, and a legible engagement model establish credibility.
- Do not mix student recruiting and partner acquisition into one call to action.
- Do not publish anonymous claims that sound larger than the evidence. Small, precise proof looks more professional than inflated language.
- Do not ship inaccessible interaction as an acceptable cost of visual polish.

## What could not be verified

The review used current public pages and first-party descriptions. It verified content order, labels, published copy, image descriptions, visible media controls, project facts, and case-study structure. It did not verify frame-by-frame homepage animation, source-level rendering methods, performance budgets, analytics, conversion results, or reduced-motion implementations. Recommendations about those areas are inferences based on MECG's needs, not observations about competitor internals.
